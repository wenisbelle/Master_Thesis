## 1. Analysis of the 50-run sets (no code changed)

Sets analysed: `multiple_runs_hand_tuned/200/{6,8,10,12}` for naive, egocentric and swarm.

- **The mean uncertainty is set by the number of drones in the air, not by the waiting.**
  - For t ≥ 10000 s, uncertainty × N × air fraction is the same for the three systems,
    within 0.4% at every N.
  - At 12 drones, egocentric spends 11.34% of drone time on the pads and 0.66% waiting.
    Swarm spends 12.10% and 0.09%: it waits less, but it charges more, so it has slightly
    less airtime.
- **Swarm returns early at every return.**
  - When a drone decided to return, it was always below the swarm mean, by about 0.28.
    So the b term was a near-constant +0.08 on the margin, not coordination.
  - Swarm charged 4.03 times per drone per 10⁴ s against 3.78 for egocentric.
  - Landing battery: egocentric 0.22, stored swarm 0.27, swarm with the cells shift 0.32.
- **Cycles.** The period is one battery cycle (about 2500–2700 s).
  - The common start at t = 0 creates the first wave.
  - The waiting room then re-bunches the drones into back-to-back groups.
  - The c term only delays a drone; nothing pulled one earlier into an empty slot.
  - Swarm's only clear win was the first wave: −4.9% uncertainty, 44 of 50 seeds.
- **Steadiness is where swarm wins** (measured with the new figure of section 6, from 5000 s):
  - 12 drones: std in time of the drones mapping 0.67 (swarm) vs 0.87 (egocentric). Time
    with ≤ 9 mapping 2.0% vs 8.7%. Mean mapping 10.53 vs 10.55.
  - 8 drones: std 0.83 vs 0.93 (naive 0.90). Time with ≤ 5 mapping 2.4% vs 4.1%.
  - Paired Wilcoxon p < 0.001 at both sizes.
- **The stored swarm runs in `200/` were made with the code of `cd307d2`**, where the cells
  margin was fixed and the `cells_*` genes did nothing. The code of `fa2de0b` diverges from
  them at about 1920 s.

## 4. Early pull

You asked for this, to send a drone to an empty base before a predicted congestion.

### 4.1 The rule

- **Natural landing.** t_B = now + (battery − `BASE_CHARGE_MARGIN`) / `DISCHARGE_RATE`. It
  does not depend on where the drone will decide: the flight home drains like the mapping.
  Egocentric, with a fixed margin of 0.25, landed at 0.220 ± 0.004 over 140 landings from
  anywhere on the map.
- **Landing now.** t_A = now + `time_to_base(position)`.
- **The pull.** When the conditions below hold, the base margin rises by c: one place in line,
  a charge / Nc of waiting, about 0.05 of battery.
- **Conditions:**
  - **Rank rule:** the drone is the *first* that would wait at t_B. Exactly Nc others are on the
    base then, all landed before it (first come, first served; a tie goes to the lower id).
    With more than Nc, an earlier drone would already wait and the pull is its own.
  - A pad is expected free at t_A.
  - t_B − t_A ≤ `SWARM_WINDOW`.
  - Both instants count the guessed landings of MAPPING rows too, so the difference is not biased.
- **Rank by landing, not position.** The belief table holds no positions, so the rank uses the
  predicted landings. The first in line is also the drone that gives up the least battery.
- **Where it acts.** The pull only acts on the base margin. The cells margin accepts the
  argument and ignores it.

### 4.2 Commitment, and its release

- **Without a commitment**, 4 of 5 pulled drones turned back within a minute: a base
  heartbeat or an encounter changed the forecast.
  - 2 of those had a pad still free, and the rank rule had flipped.
  - 2 found the base full.
- **The rule now.** A pulled drone keeps going to the base (`committed_to_base`) while a pad is
  still expected free when it lands, counted from the known drones (`pull_still_holds`).
- **When the base turns out full**, the commitment ends and the drone decides again. With c it
  goes back to mapping if its battery allows (your request).
- **At an encounter** the other drone's pull travels in the goto message (`early_pull`).

## 5. Charge time `100 + (1 − battery) · 300` s

- **The formula.** A charge lasts `RECHARGE_FIXED_TIME + (1 − battery) · RECHARGE_TIME_PER_BATTERY`,
  with the battery the drone lands with. The battery does not change on the ground. Before it
  was a flat `TIME_TO_RECHARGE` = 300 s.
- **Examples.** 325 s at 0.25, 352 s at 0.16, 250 s at 0.50.
- **Scope.** All 7 Analytical systems. MARL is not changed (your instruction); it still
  hard-codes 100 s.
- **What an early return costs now.** 1% of battery is about 30 s of flight but only 3 s of charge.
- **Inputs and outputs.**
  - The drones and the bases get the two constants from `main.py`.
  - The recorders store `recharge_fixed_time` and `recharge_time_per_battery` instead of `time_to_recharge`.

- **What the plan books.** Every charge is booked as `PLANNED_CHARGE_TIME` = 350 s (your choice)

- **Why it is safe.** 350 s covers any drone landing above 0.17, so a pad frees at or before
  the end of its slot. Booked waiting-room starts come from the base, with the real durations.

## 6. Drones-mapping figure

- **The new figure.** [plot_multiple_runs.py](plot_multiple_runs.py) draws `<name>_mapping`:
  - [drones_mapping:563](plot_multiple_runs.py#L563) computes the series;
  - [plot_mapping:573](plot_multiple_runs.py#L573) draws the figure;
  - it is wired in at [:1011](plot_multiple_runs.py#L1011).
- **What it counts.** Drones mapping = drones − charging − waiting − dead, from the series the
  runs already store. GOING_TO_BASE is not logged apart, so a drone flying to the base counts
  as mapping; its camera still sees cells.
- **The panels:**
  - **(a)** the mean number mapping in time, in `--window` windows;
  - **(b)** the share of the time with at most k drones mapping, log scale;
  - **(c)** each run's standard deviation in time of the number mapping, paired Wilcoxon with
    two systems seeded alike.
- **Steady state.** (b) and (c) take the time from `--steady-state` on.
- **Colours.** Okabe-Ito with line styles and markers.
- **Example:** `python3 Analytical/plot_multiple_runs.py --system egocentric swarm --hand-tuned --range 200 --drones 12 --steady-state 5000`
